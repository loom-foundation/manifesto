# Manifesto Authoring Rules

## Purpose

This document captures the principles governing the development and maintenance of `../manifesto.md`.

The manifesto is the **philosophical surface** of Loom, the worldview through which the methodology makes sense. It is what readers carry away first; everything else is structured to honour it. Edits to the manifesto are rare, deliberate, and constrained by the principles below.

## Preserve the poetic register

The manifesto speaks in a **poetic, declarative, restrained** register. Short lines. Strong claims. Named metaphors. Edits must preserve this voice. The manifesto's job is to inspire and orient, not to procedure.

**How to apply.** Before adding prose to the manifesto, read it aloud. If it sounds like methodology or documentation, it belongs elsewhere.

## Resonance is the substance

In a manifesto, resonance *is* the substance. Two sentences that carry the same semantic load to an analytical reader can strike different chords in the chest, and both earn their place.

Never merge or cut manifesto sentences on grounds of semantic redundancy alone. Ask which dimension of human experience each sentence speaks to. For example: "We never let the tool constrain what we dare to imagine" and "The question is never what the tool permits, but what the work demands" read as one claim to an engineer. They are not. The first guards the imagination from the intellectual dominance of the tool; the second is the governing question of the work, whose demands reach beyond the intellectual.

## Defensibility is the wrong optimisation target

Pitches and manifestos make bold claims without justifying them; not from carelessness, but from purpose. An unexplained claim catches in the mind of the curious and the dubious alike; both want to know if it holds. That friction is the opening: the manifesto earns the conversation, and the conversation is where the claim gets backed. A caveat bolted onto a bold line closes that opening.

## Omission is craft

The manifesto is deliberate in what it leaves unsaid. The smart reader infers, and the inference is the reader's own act of commitment. For example, in the Nirvana Shatakam, Adi Shankara omits deliberately, in service of the meter and the awakening. Only a pedant scores omission as error. Slight omissions and inversions interrupt habitual, mechanical reading and make the reader pause, and the pause is where a reader drops from parsing into feeling.

## Inversion earns its puzzlement

"What the loom weaves, the mind must first wear" is deliberately impossible imagery. The reader must resolve the inversion, and the resolution is the thesis: to wear the cloth before it is woven is to hold the finished thing whole in the mind. Readers remember what they had to complete. The cloth is worn twice in the manifesto: first by the mind, last by the world ("Fabric unworn has no purpose"), and the two wearings bracket the entire model.

## Buried depth is deliberate

Some lines are precise in ways most readers will not catch: "alters the pattern in the chain" (the warp chain: fix upstream, never patch the cloth), "The reed beats every pick true before the next is thrown" (verification inside the making; validation from the world). These are research hooks that reward the curious with the history of programming, machines, and the mills.

## Be true to the craft you borrow

The manifesto is built on the Jacquard loom, so it invites — and must survive — loom-literate scrutiny. Every weaving detail is load-bearing: warp and weft are thread systems, not "two threads"; a pick is beaten before the next is thrown; the sett is the plan and the reed keeps it. Verify craft claims against the craft, not against what sounds right.

## One meaning per word; one concern per belief

Where the manifesto assigns meanings, they must agree everywhere: intent is the Why, requirements the What, specification the How — so the warp is labelled by the thread it is (Requirement), and Intent is what enters the loom. Each belief guards exactly one axis. A sentence too big for its belief is promoted, not crammed.

## Read the whole cloth

Sentence-level analysis deceives: an edit can look right at the swatch and reveal its seam only against the light of the whole. After any change, re-read the manifesto entire and cold. The felt reading of the whole outranks the theorised arc.

## The capitalisation device

The manifesto capitalises by crystallisation: lowercase while becoming, capitalised once become. "Vision becomes intent" (still forming) but "The loom weaves Intent into product" (handed over whole). Do not "fix" the asymmetry, it is deliberate.

Product never takes the capital: by belief 5 the product never finishes becoming. One capital is the right number; rare is what makes it felt.
